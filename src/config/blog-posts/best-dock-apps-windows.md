# The Best Dock and Sidebar Apps for Windows (2026): An Honest List

Want a macOS-style dock or a slide-out sidebar on Windows? Here's an honest comparison of Winstep Nexus, MyDockFinder, Seelen UI, the classic docks and CoolDesk — including what each one does better than the rest.

The Windows taskbar is fine at one thing: showing what's open. But if you've used a Mac, you miss the Dock — and if you juggle several projects, you probably wish your apps, folders and links for *this* piece of work lived somewhere one glance away. That's the gap dock and sidebar apps fill.

They come in two flavors. **Eye-candy docks** recreate the macOS Dock — magnification, animations, a bar of icons at the bottom of the screen. **Workspace docks and sidebars** care less about looks and more about what's inside: the right apps, files and links for what you're doing right now.

## Winstep Nexus

Nexus has been the go-to Windows dock for years, and it's still actively maintained. You get a bottom-centered bar with magnification, reflections, running-app indicators, and an enormous amount of customization.

**What it's best at:** making a dock *yours*. Themes, icon effects, sizes, behaviors — if you can imagine a dock setting, Nexus probably has it. The free version is generous.

**Where others beat it:** the depth is also the downside — the settings can feel like a cockpit. The most advanced features (multiple docks, tabbed docks) are in the paid Nexus Ultimate. And like every classic dock, it's a launcher for icons you pin by hand; it doesn't know which apps belong to which project.

## MyDockFinder

MyDockFinder is the "make Windows look like a Mac" app. Sold for a low one-time price on Steam, it recreates the macOS Dock and the menu bar at the top of the screen with uncanny accuracy — the hover magnification, the spacing, the animations.

**What it's best at:** looks. If what you miss about the Mac is how the Dock *feels*, nothing on Windows gets closer.

**Where others beat it:** it's about appearance, not organization. It's paid, it changes a lot of your desktop at once, and Windows updates occasionally break its shell tweaks until it catches up.

## Seelen UI

Seelen UI is a free, open-source desktop environment for Windows: a dock, a customizable top bar, an app launcher, and a tiling window manager that arranges your windows for you.

**What it's best at:** ambition. If you want to rebuild your whole Windows desktop — Linux-style tiling, a status bar, a dock — in one free tool, Seelen is the most complete option.

**Where others beat it:** it's a lot of change at once, and it rewards people who enjoy tweaking. If you just want a dock, a dedicated dock is simpler.

## RocketDock and ObjectDock (the classics)

RocketDock and Stardock's ObjectDock are where Windows docks started, and you'll still see them recommended.

**What they're best at:** simplicity, and in ObjectDock's case Stardock's polish.

**Where others beat them:** RocketDock hasn't been meaningfully updated in many years and shows it on modern high-DPI displays. ObjectDock is paid and has seen far less attention than Stardock's other apps. For a new setup in 2026, start with Nexus instead.

## CoolDesk (that's us)

CoolDesk is a free project workspace for Windows and macOS, and its dock is built around one idea: show what belongs to the work you're doing right now. One shortcut — **Ctrl+Shift+D** (⌘+Shift+D on Mac) — cycles between three layouts:

- **Full window** — the complete CoolDesk workspace view.
- **Side dock** — a slim handle on the left or right edge of your screen. Click it and a sidebar slides in over your apps with your workspaces, tabs and notes; click away and it tucks back to the edge. Nothing on your screen gets resized.
- **Bottom bar** — a Mac-style dock strip. One view shows everything open right now (running apps and browser tabs); the other shows the current project's links, apps, folders and files. Drop a file from your file manager onto the bar to add it to the project.

Folder chips are developer-aware: they show the project's stack logo, the git branch and whether it has uncommitted changes, and when a dev server is running.

**What it's best at:** context. Every other dock on this list shows the same icons no matter what you're working on. CoolDesk's dock changes with your project — switch workspace and the bar fills with that project's apps, folders and links. It's free, needs no account, and keeps everything on your device.

**Where others beat it:** looks and customization. There's no magnification or icon reflections — Nexus and MyDockFinder win on eye candy. It doesn't replace the Windows taskbar or re-skin your desktop the way Seelen UI does. And it's the youngest tool here.

## What about a "sidebar" like Windows 7 gadgets?

If by sidebar you mean the old Windows 7 gadgets — clocks, weather, CPU meters — that's a different category. **GadgetPack** (formerly 8GadgetPack) brings the original gadgets back, and **Rainmeter** is the free, open-source standard for desktop widgets and skins. Both are still maintained, and both work alongside any dock on this list.

## FAQ

**Can I get the macOS Dock on Windows 11?**
Not the real one, but MyDockFinder copies it closely, and Winstep Nexus gives you the same idea with far more customization.

**What's the best free dock for Windows?**
Winstep Nexus (free version) for a classic dock, Seelen UI if you also want a top bar and tiling, and CoolDesk if you want a dock organized by project.

**Can I add a sidebar to Windows 11?**
Yes. CoolDesk's side dock slides in from the screen edge with your workspaces, tabs and notes. For gadget-style widgets, use GadgetPack or Rainmeter.

**Will a dock app slow down my PC?**
Modern docks are light, but stacking several shell tweaks at once is where problems start. Pick one dock and one widget tool at most.
