# The Best Sidebar Apps for Mac and Windows (2026): An Honest List

Want a sidebar on the edge of your screen for your apps, files and links? Here's an honest comparison of Sidebar, EdgeFlow, Yoink, SideSlide, WindowSill and CoolDesk, including what each one does better than the rest.

A sidebar app puts the things you reach for most on the edge of your screen, one flick of the mouse away. It doesn't sit at the bottom like the Dock or the taskbar, and it hides when you don't need it. It's the fastest way to get around your computer that doesn't involve typing.

Sidebar apps come in three flavors. **Dock replacements** move your app launcher to a vertical bar on the side. **Shelves** appear at the edge only when you're dragging something, as a place to park files. **Workspace sidebars** show the apps, files and links for whatever you're working on right now.

## Sidebar (Mac)

Sidebar is a Dock replacement: it moves your apps to a vertical bar on the side of the screen, where widescreen displays have room to spare. Hover over an app to see previews of all its windows, group apps and folders into Stacks, and press the Sidebar button for a Start-menu-style launcher. It runs on macOS 13 Ventura or newer and works across multiple displays.

**What it's best at:** being a better Dock. If you like the Dock but want it vertical, with window previews and a launcher, this is the most complete option on the Mac.

**Where others beat it:** it's paid (a lifetime licence or a small subscription) and it's about apps and windows. Your browser tabs, notes and the links for a project don't live in it.

## EdgeFlow (Mac)

EdgeFlow is a small, inexpensive launcher that slides out when you hover over the left or right edge of the screen. It launches apps, opens websites and runs Apple Shortcuts, and you reorder items by dragging them.

**What it's best at:** simplicity at a low one-time price. If you want a handful of shortcuts at the screen edge and nothing else, EdgeFlow does exactly that.

**Where others beat it:** it's new and does one job. There are no window previews like Sidebar has, no folders or files, and it doesn't change with what you're working on.

## Yoink (Mac)

Yoink is a different kind of sidebar. It's a shelf that fades in at the edge of your screen the moment you start dragging a file or a piece of content. Drop it there, switch apps or Spaces, then drag it out again where you need it. It's been refined for over a decade and is on the Mac App Store.

**What it's best at:** drag and drop. Moving files between full-screen apps, Spaces or windows that overlap gets much easier.

**Where others beat it:** it's a temporary holding area, not a launcher. You don't open apps or navigate from it. Pair it with one of the other tools here rather than choosing it instead of them.

## SideSlide (Windows)

SideSlide is a long-running free desktop sidebar for Windows. It docks to any screen edge and slides out to show containers you fill with shortcuts, folders, web links, notes and RSS feeds. It's still updated regularly and comes in a portable version you can run from a USB drive.

**What it's best at:** flexibility for free. You can build almost any kind of slide-out panel and organize it into as many containers as you like.

**Where others beat it:** the interface looks dated, and everything is arranged by hand. It doesn't know which project you're on or what's open in your browser.

## WindowSill (Windows)

WindowSill isn't a sidebar in the strict sense. It's a slim command bar for Windows 10 and 11 that sits along the taskbar, inspired by the MacBook Touch Bar. The free version includes clipboard history, media controls, reminders, file conversion and extensions. AI writing tools are in a paid WindowSill+ tier. It's on the Microsoft Store.

**What it's best at:** small tools you'd otherwise open separate apps for, always one click away.

**Where others beat it:** it's for quick actions, not for getting to your apps and files. For a launcher on the edge of the screen, SideSlide or CoolDesk fits better.

## CoolDesk (that's us)

CoolDesk is a free project workspace for Mac and Windows, and its side dock is built around one idea: show what belongs to the work you're doing right now. Press **Ctrl+Shift+D** (⌘+Shift+D on Mac) to switch to the side dock. A slim handle sits on the left or right edge of the screen. Hover or click and a sidebar slides in over your apps with your workspaces, open tabs and notes. Click away and it tucks back to the edge, and nothing on your screen gets resized.

Each workspace holds a project's links, apps, folders and files. Switch workspace and the sidebar fills with that project's things. Your browser tabs show up alongside your desktop apps. On a Mac the sidebar also works over full-screen apps and on whichever display your cursor is on.

**What it's best at:** context. Every other sidebar here shows the same shortcuts no matter what you're working on. CoolDesk's changes with your project, and it puts browser tabs and desktop apps in one place. It's free, needs no account, and keeps everything on your device.

**Where others beat it:** it isn't a Dock replacement with window previews (that's Sidebar), it isn't a drag-and-drop shelf (that's Yoink), and it has fewer looks and layout options than SideSlide. It's also the youngest tool here.

## What about browser sidebars?

If by "sidebar" you mean one *inside your browser*, that's a different category. **Vivaldi** and **Opera** have built-in sidebars for web panels and messengers. **Microsoft Edge** is removing the apps from its sidebar (Copilot stays), so if you relied on them it's worth finding a replacement now. Browser sidebars only work inside the browser. The apps on this list work everywhere on your computer.

## FAQ

**Can I put the Mac Dock on the side of the screen?**
Yes. In System Settings → Desktop & Dock, set "Position on screen" to Left or Right. For window previews and a launcher as well, try Sidebar. For a sidebar organized by project, try CoolDesk.

**What's the best free sidebar app for Windows 11?**
SideSlide for a classic slide-out sidebar, WindowSill for a free command bar, and CoolDesk if you want a sidebar organized by project.

**What's the best free sidebar app for Mac?**
CoolDesk is free. Most other Mac sidebar apps are paid, though they're inexpensive: EdgeFlow costs a few dollars, and Sidebar and Yoink offer free trials.

**Will a sidebar get in the way of my windows?**
Not if it auto-hides. Sidebar, EdgeFlow and CoolDesk all tuck away to the screen edge, and CoolDesk's side dock slides over your apps without resizing them.
