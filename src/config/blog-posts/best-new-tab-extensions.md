# The Best New Tab Extensions in 2026 (and When to Use Each)

Your new tab is the most-visited screen in your browser — most people hit it fifty to a hundred times a day. Replacing the default blank page is one of the highest-leverage changes you can make to how your browser feels. Here's an honest rundown of the main options, and which kind of person each one actually fits.

## Momentum — the calm, beautiful dashboard

Momentum turns your new tab into a daily ritual: a full-bleed photo backdrop, a greeting, one "main focus" for today, and a simple to-do list. It doesn't try to organize your work — it tries to make the moment you open a tab feel calmer.

**Pick Momentum if** you want your new tab to feel serene more than structured, and a single daily focus prompt is the habit you're after. Richer features (recurring to-dos, integrations, more themes) sit behind a Momentum Plus subscription.

## Toby — visual tab collections

Toby replaces your new tab with a grid of saved tab collections — drag a tab in, reopen it later, share a collection with your team. It's simple, visual, and has a big loyal following, especially for people managing many reference links.

**Pick Toby if** the core job is filing and reopening groups of links, especially with a team.

## Workona — cloud-synced browser workspaces

Workona popularized the "browser workspace" idea: spaces that hold tabs, docs and tasks, auto-saved and synced to the cloud. It's built for teams and for people who work across multiple machines.

**Pick Workona if** your whole workflow lives in the browser and needs to follow you across devices, or your team collaborates in shared spaces.

## CoolDesk — project workspaces with a launcher built in

CoolDesk takes a different angle: the new tab isn't just a dashboard or a link collection, it's a project. Tabs, desktop apps, links and notes get grouped per project, and a spotlight search (Alt+K) reaches across all of it — open tabs, browser history, bookmarks, notes and running desktop apps — not just what you've deliberately saved. Everything is free, local-first, and works the same on Windows and macOS.

**Pick CoolDesk if** you're juggling several projects at once and want the whole context — not just tabs — to come back together with one keystroke, or you want a launcher and a new tab to be the same tool instead of two separate ones.

## Which one is actually right for you?

- Want the prettiest new tab and a light daily habit? **Momentum.**
- Managing lots of saved links, possibly with a team? **Toby.**
- Need cloud-synced browser workspaces across devices? **Workona.**
- Juggling multiple active projects — tabs, apps, notes — and want one search bar for all of it? **CoolDesk.**

None of these are mutually exclusive with your browser's built-in bookmarks or history — they're just better front doors. Pick the one whose job matches the problem you actually have, not the one with the prettiest screenshot.
