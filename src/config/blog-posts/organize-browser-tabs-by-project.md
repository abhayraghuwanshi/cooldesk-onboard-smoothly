# How to Organize Browser Tabs by Project (Instead of 20 Open Windows)

The best way to organize browser tabs is by project: one saved group per piece of work, which you can close without fear and reopen in seconds. Here's a simple system that works — with your browser's built-in tools, or with a free workspace app.

If you have twenty windows open, it isn't a discipline problem. Tabs are the only "save" button most people trust. Closing a tab feels like losing it, so tabs pile up as a to-do list, a reading list and a filing cabinet all at once. The browser was never designed for that job.

The fix isn't fewer tabs. It's giving each project a home, so closing a tab stops feeling risky.

## The system in three rules

1. **One project, one group.** Every client, feature, research topic or course gets its own named group. Not per window, not per day — per project.
2. **Saving is closing.** When you step away from a project, its tabs go into its group and close. Your browser stays light; nothing is lost.
3. **Finding beats filing.** You shouldn't need to remember where you saved something. You should be able to type a word and get it back.

Any tool that supports those three rules works. Here are the main options.

## Option 1: Chrome's built-in tab groups (no install)

Right-click a tab → **Add tab to new group**, name it after the project, and pick a color. Saved groups appear in your bookmarks bar and sync through your Google account, so you can close a group and reopen it later.

**Good for:** a few projects, all in the browser, no new software.
**Where it falls short:** you drag tabs into groups one at a time, groups only hold tabs (not your notes, files or desktop apps), and there's no search across them. See [CoolDesk vs Chrome tab groups](/vs/chrome-tab-groups) for a full comparison.

## Option 2: A browser workspace extension

Tools like Workona and Toby replace your new tab with a home for saved tab groups. Workona organizes tabs into cloud-synced workspaces; Toby files them into visual collections.

**Good for:** heavy browser users, teams sharing collections, and anyone who needs their groups on several computers.
**Where it falls short:** both need an account for sync, and they stop at the browser — your project's desktop apps and files live somewhere else. Our [new tab extension comparison](/blog/best-new-tab-extensions) covers all the options side by side.

## Option 3: Project workspaces with CoolDesk (free)

CoolDesk gives each project a workspace that holds its tabs and links *and* its desktop apps, folders, files and notes. Here's the setup:

**1. Install it.** [Download the desktop app](/api/download) and add [the browser extension](/api/extension). Your new tab becomes your workspace home.

**2. Create a workspace per project.** Name it after the work — "Acme redesign", "Thesis", "Q4 hiring".

**3. Add what's already open.** Use the add button to pick from your open tabs and put them in the right workspace. You can also drag folders and files onto a workspace to add them.

**4. Let AI do the first pass.** If you're starting from chaos, ask CoolDesk's AI to group your recent browsing by project, then tidy the result instead of filing everything by hand.

**5. Close tabs freely.** They're saved in the workspace. When you need one again, press **Alt+K** and type a word — CoolDesk searches your open tabs, saved workspaces, history, bookmarks, apps and notes together, and jumps to a tab that's already open instead of opening a duplicate.

**Good for:** people juggling several projects at once, whose work is split across the browser *and* desktop apps.
**Where it falls short:** there's no cloud sync — everything stays on your device, by design. If your groups must follow you across computers, Workona does that better.

## Habits that keep it working

- **Name groups after outcomes, not websites.** "Launch landing page" ages better than "Webflow stuff".
- **End the day by closing.** Spend 30 seconds closing everything that's already saved in a workspace. Tomorrow starts with a clean browser.
- **Archive finished projects.** A project you're done with should leave your main view, not sit in a list forever.
- **If tab chaos is a daily struggle** — and for many people with ADHD it is — make the system forgiving. Searching for a tab you didn't file is fine. The goal is never losing things, not perfect organization.

## FAQ

**What's the best free way to organize tabs by project?**
Chrome's tab groups if you only need tabs. CoolDesk if you also want your apps, files and notes grouped with them, plus one search for all of it. Both are free.

**How do I stop having 20+ browser windows open?**
Give each project a saved group, and close tabs as soon as they're saved. The windows pile up because closing feels risky — once everything has a home, it doesn't.

**Will my tabs be lost if my browser crashes?**
Tabs saved in a workspace or a saved tab group survive crashes. For recovering whatever happened to be open, a session saver like Session Buddy is also worth having — see [CoolDesk vs Session Buddy](/vs/session-buddy).

**Does this work in Edge or Brave?**
Yes — CoolDesk's extension works in Chrome and in Chromium-based browsers like Edge and Brave.
