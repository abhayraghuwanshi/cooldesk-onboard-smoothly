# Arc Is Shutting Down: The Best Arc Browser Alternatives (2026)

Arc stops working on December 22, 2026. Here's what's happening, how to get your data out, and an honest comparison of Dia, Zen, Helium, Vivaldi, Crest, Microsoft Edge and CoolDesk, including what each one does better than the rest.

On October 22, 2026, The Browser Company announced that Arc will shut down. This isn't the "no new features" maintenance mode of the past year: on **December 22** the desktop apps for Mac and Windows stop working, and Arc accounts are deactivated. The company says keeping a browser secure takes more and more work, and it's putting everything into its newer browser, Dia.

If Arc's Spaces and sidebar changed how you browse, you have two months to pick something else.

Full disclosure: **we make one of the tools on this list** (CoolDesk). So the usual rule applies: for every tool, including ours, we say what it's genuinely best at *and* where another tool beats it.

## The Arc shutdown timeline

- **Now:** no new Arc accounts. Existing installs keep working and still get basic updates.
- **November 23:** Arc Search on iPhone and Android loses its AI features (including Browse for Me), sign-in and sync. Desktop users start seeing shutdown reminders.
- **December 15:** last day to request a copy of your account data, or its deletion.
- **December 22:** Arc for Mac and Windows stops working and accounts are deactivated. Account data is deleted within 30 days.

## Before you switch: get your data out

Do this first, whichever browser you choose, and keep Arc installed until you've checked everything made it across.

1. **Export your Spaces and tabs.** In Arc's settings, under **Advanced**, you can export your Spaces and tabs as a list of links. That's your safety net for any browser.
2. **Use the new browser's importer.** Most browsers on this list can import Arc's bookmarks, history and passwords. Dia's importer also brings over open tabs, extensions and cookies, turns Spaces into Profiles and folders into pinned Tab Groups.
3. **Know what won't move.** Easels, Boosts and other Arc-only features have no equivalent anywhere. Saved payment methods and autofill data don't transfer either. Nested folders become flat groups in Dia.

## Dia

Dia is The Browser Company's new browser and the official answer to "where do Arc users go?" Your Spaces become Profiles, folders become pinned Tab Groups, and passwords, bookmarks, history, extensions and open tabs come across through its importer. Arc's browser features are free in Dia. Its AI assistant, which can read the page you're on and work across tabs, is optional.

**What it's best at:** the easiest move. Same company, a built-in importer, and many Arc ideas carried over.

**Where others beat it:** Dia is built around AI, and it's a different browser from Arc. Many Arc favorites (Easels, Boosts, nested folders) are gone. If you use the AI, page content can be sent to outside AI providers, and sharing content to improve Dia is on by default (you can turn it off). And it's the same company that's shutting Arc down, which may give you pause.

## Zen

Zen is a free, open-source browser built on Firefox, and the most popular "Arc, but open" option. It has a vertical tab sidebar, isolated **Workspaces**, split view with up to four tabs in a grid, **Glance** (a quick-look preview for links, like Arc's Peek) and a Compact Mode that hides everything but the page.

**What it's best at:** feeling like Arc. If you want the sidebar, Spaces and split view without Chromium or a company behind it, start here.

**Where others beat it:** it's Firefox underneath, so Chrome extensions don't work. Use Firefox add-ons instead. A few sites built only for Chrome can be rough. It's also a community project, so polish varies between releases.

## Helium

Helium is a minimal, privacy-first browser built on ungoogled-chromium, with **uBlock Origin built in**, no analytics and no background calls to Google. It has vertical tabs, split view and !bang shortcuts in the address bar, and it runs Chrome extensions. It has grown fast, passing 20,000 GitHub stars within about 18 months.

**What it's best at:** speed and privacy with zero setup. Ad blocking that works out of the box, in a browser that stays out of your way.

**Where others beat it:** no sync and no built-in password manager, on purpose. It also has nothing like Arc's Spaces, so it's a calmer Chrome, not an Arc replacement.

## Vivaldi

Vivaldi is the browser for people who like to tune everything. It has Workspaces, tab stacks, tab tiling (split view), web panels in a sidebar, and a built-in ad blocker, mail client, calendar and notes. It's Chromium-based, so Chrome extensions work, and it's been around for a decade.

**What it's best at:** features and control. Almost anything Arc did, Vivaldi can be configured to do.

**Where others beat it:** it's the opposite of Arc's calm design. The settings can be overwhelming, and making it *look* like Arc takes work.

## Crest

Crest is a new, open-source (MPL 2.0) browser made for Arc refugees on Apple devices: Spaces, a sidebar, Peek and split view, on Mac, iPad and iPhone, synced through iCloud. It runs Chrome extensions and can import from Arc, Zen, Chrome, Safari and Firefox.

**What it's best at:** an Arc-style browser that also works on your iPhone and iPad, now that Arc Search is fading.

**Where others beat it:** it's in early access and its security hasn't been independently audited yet, so it's not a safe choice for your main browser at work. It's Apple-only.

## Microsoft Edge

Edge often gets overlooked, but it has vertical tabs, Workspaces for grouping tabs, and split screen built in. It runs Chrome extensions and is already installed on every Windows PC.

**What it's best at:** getting most of Arc's layout with no new install, especially on Windows or a work machine where you can't choose your browser.

**Where others beat it:** it's busy, with lots of Microsoft prompts and shopping features to switch off, and it has none of Arc's design sense.

## FAQ

**When does Arc stop working?**
December 22, 2026, on both Mac and Windows. After that, the desktop app no longer opens and Arc accounts are deactivated.

**Will I lose my Arc data?**
Not if you export it first. Arc's settings (under Advanced) can export your Spaces and tabs as links, and most browsers can import Arc's bookmarks, history and passwords. Account data is deleted within 30 days of December 22.

**What's the closest browser to Arc?**
Zen for the look and feel, and Dia for the most direct migration.

**Is Dia free?**
Yes. Arc's browser features are free in Dia, and its AI features are optional.

**Can I keep using Chrome and still have Arc-style Spaces?**
